# PC Objectives — Difficulty, Danger, and Notoriety

## Principle

**Players** define their objectives (formally or informally). The **GM** evaluates difficulty and puts obstacles in their way. Never the reverse.

---

## Axis 1 — Difficulty Tiers (number of obstacles)

| Tier | Name | Scale | Examples (indicative — players choose) | Obstacle Layers |
|--------|-----|---------|------------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| **0** | Daily life | Individual, hours-days | Pay a toll, navigate around a fallen tree, pay a merchant, repair a tool, get a tavern keeper to talk | Micro-obstacles — no vital danger, narrative texture and daily choices |
| **1** | Survival | Individual, days | Survive the winter, tend a wound, find food | 1-2 (hunger, cold, injury) |
| **2** | Settlement | A location, weeks | Build a camp, attract the first settlers, secure a zone | 2-3 (resources, labor, local threats) |
| **3** | Influence | Local, months | Become an authority, negotiate an alliance, build a village | 3-5 (factions, legitimacy, logistics, conflicts) |
| **4** | Domination | Regional, years | Become sovereign, unify factions, build a kingdom | 5-8 (wars, betrayals, winter, ancient secrets) |
| **5** | Legendary | Trans-regional, years+ | Become a myth, awaken/pacify an ancient force, change the course of history | 8+ (supernatural forces, continental repercussions, sacrifices) |

## Axis 2 — Danger (physical risk, independent of difficulty)

An obstacle can be **difficult without being dangerous** (a month of diplomacy) or **dangerous without being difficult** (crossing a venomous marsh in 2 minutes).

| Level | Risk on failure | Examples |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| 🟢 **Green** — Safe | Loss of time, money, or opportunity. No physical risk. | Town, allied territory, formal negotiation, sheltered work |
| 🟡 **Yellow** — Risky | Minor injury, fatigue, material loss, reputation damage. | Hostile frontier, neutral terrain, tense negotiation, travel through known areas |
| 🟠 **Orange** — Dangerous | Serious injury, sickness, loss of a companion, lasting consequences. | Enemy territory, creature, trap, cave descent, swift waters |
| 🔴 **Red** — Deadly | Probable or certain death if failure is total. Sacrifice necessary. | Unequal combat, slow poison, supernatural element, fall, exposure without protection |

### Combining difficulty and danger — examples

| Situation | Tier | Danger | Tension comes from… |
|-----------|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| Pay a toll to a bandit checkpoint | 0 🟢 | 🟡 Yellow — if without money, the band might get upset | Social risk |
| Negotiate a truce with a faction leader | 3 🔴 | 🟡 Yellow — possible captivity, not immediate death | Complexity of power dynamics |
| Cross the forest in winter | 1 🟠 | 🟠 Orange — cold kills, creatures prowl | Immediate survival |
| Duel against a great house champion | 2 🟠 | 🔴 Red — probable death if you lose | Real threat of death |
| Convince a discrete and elusive faction | 4 ⚫ | 🟢 Green — they don't kill over words | Patience and timelines |

### ⚠️ Pitfall — Always balance danger
If everything is red, nothing is red. Alternate levels. 🔴 combat → 🟢 respite → 🟡 negotiation → 🟠 exploration. Contrast creates tension.

## Axis 3 — Notoriety/Importance (consequence multiplier)

The notoriety of a **person** and the importance of a **faction** modulate the repercussions of PC actions. A threatened peasant does not carry the same weight as a threatened count.

### Notoriety of individuals (PCs and NPCs)

| Level | Access | Repercussions if you target them |
|--------|-------|----------------------------------------|
| 🔘 **Unknown** | No privileged access. Lines, standard prices, indifferent glances. | No one will hunt you. No one will avenge them. |
| 🔵 **Locally known** | Access to certain circles. The tavern keeper recognizes you, the smith gives you credit. | Local echo. Threatening a notable = their family and neighbors hunt you. |
| 🟣 **Important** | Access to high circles. Merchants offer favorable terms. | Every action carries weight. Attack = response from factions protecting them. |
| ⚪ **Legendary** | Nearly unlimited access. Kings receive you. | Every gesture is observed, reported. Impossible to stay hidden long-term. |

### Importance of factions

| Level | Reach | Example |
|--------|--------|---------|
| 🔘 **Discrete** | Invisible to common folk | A secret order, hidden guardians |
| 🔵 **Local** | A village, a valley, a ford | A local bandit gang |
| 🟣 **Regional** | Several counties, a province | A noble house, counts |
| ⚪ **Major** | The kingdom / the continent | A large merchant company |

### How notoriety modifies obstacles

| If you target… | Difficulty | Danger | Why |
|----------------------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| An isolated bandit (🔘 unknown) | Tier 1 | 🟡 Yellow | No repercussions, no allies. Just combat. |
| A bandit lieutenant (🔵 locally known) | Tier 2 | 🟠 Orange | His men will hunt you. The leader will know. |
| A faction leader (🟣 important) | Tier 4 | 🔴 Red | The entire faction + their allies have accounts with them. War assured. |
| A regional lord (🟣 regional) | Tier 5 | 🔴 Red | State apparatus. Army, investigators, manhunt. |

### Threshold rule — minimum required notoriety

Certain obstacles are **inaccessible** if the PC lacks sufficient notoriety:

| PC Notoriety | Can do… | Cannot do… |
|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| 🔘 Unknown | Speak to a guard, enter a town | Audience with a Count, invitation to a noble's home |
| 🔵 Locally known | Meet a faction lieutenant | Negotiate as equals with the leader |
| 🟣 Important | Negotiate with leaders, defy authority | Summon multiple leaders to your table |
| ⚪ Legendary | Change the rules, summon anyone | Act in the shadows |

### ⚠️ Pitfall — Notoriety forgotten in consequences
If a PC attacks an important NPC and no one reacts → inconsistency. The target's notoriety determines the scope of the response:
- 🔘 Unknown → no vengeance
- 🔵 Locally known → organized reprisal (their family, associates)
- 🟣 Important → entire faction response, diplomatic repercussions
- ⚪ Legendary → open war, continent-wide reactions

## Sources of obstacles (all axes combined)

- **Daily life (tier 0)** — tolls, merchants, broken tools, weather, rumors, locked doors, formalities
- **Factions (tier 1+)** — hostility, competition, alliances (cf. faction-tracking.md)
- **Environment (tier 1+)** — winter, terrain, forest, creatures, seasons
- **Characters (tier 1+)** — rivalries, betrayals, conflicting loyalties
- **GM secrets (tier 3+)** — revelations that overturn the situation
- **PC's past (tier 1+)** — bonuses/penalties based on previous actions
- **Time (tier 1+)** — the faction clock makes an objective harder if the PC waits

## Procedure (hidden from player)

1. The player expresses or demonstrates an objective
2. The GM evaluates the **tier** in secret
3. The GM evaluates the expected **danger**
4. The GM identifies obstacles from world state (target notoriety)
5. The GM creates clock entries, encounters, dilemmas
6. The player advances → obstacles are revealed progressively
7. If the PC succeeds at a major obstacle → reduce the apparent tier (but not remaining obstacles)

## Pitfalls

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correction |
|-----------|-------------|
| Reveal all obstacles at once | Reveal them during play — the PC must feel their way |
| Obstacle with no way out | Always leave a door (even a very narrow one) |
| Objectives defined by the GM | It's up to the player to choose what they want |
| No resistance at a high tier | The world must react to ambition |
| Excessive resistance at a low tier | Surviving winter doesn't require facing a dragon |
| Notoriety forgotten in consequences | Check the target's level before describing the response |
| Always red, never green | Alternate danger levels — contrast creates tension |
