# 🎬 Scene Output Template — Show, Don't Tell

> Positive definition of a good in-scene GM response. Read before narrating a scene.
> Companion to `references/rythme-narratif-concret.md` (one action per moment) and the
> **Player Channel** rule in `SOUL.md`. This file answers a different question: not *how many*
> actions, but *what content* is allowed to reach the player.

## Principle

The player lives the scene through their character's senses. Narrate **only what the present PCs
can perceive right now** — and **show** it (concrete sensation, observable behavior) rather than
**tell** it (your intentions, your mechanics, a character's hidden mind). If the PCs in the scene
cannot see, hear, smell, touch, taste, or directly deduce it, it does not reach the stream.

## The Template — what a good scene response contains

A good in-scene response is built from these blocks, in this order. Keep it tight; not every block
appears every turn, but **block 4 always closes the response.**

1. **Sensory anchor** — one or two concrete details the PCs perceive *now*: sight, sound, smell,
   touch, temperature, light. Specific, not abstract. (*"The lantern gutters; cold damp rises from
   the stairwell"* — not *"the atmosphere is tense"*.)
2. **Observable NPC behavior and speech** — what the NPCs visibly *do* and *say*. Posture, tone,
   gesture, gaze, hesitation. Their inner state is shown only through its outward signs, never
   stated. (*"Berthe sets down the knife and does not meet your eyes"* — never *"Berthe is hiding
   that she is afraid"*.)
3. **The current situation** — the standing facts of the scene the PCs can read: who is here, what
   is in reach, what just changed. Open options, not assigned actions (see
   `rythme-narratif-concret.md`).
4. **An opening for player action** — a clear handoff that returns control. A question, a visible
   choice, or a beat that invites a decision. *"What do you do?"*

## What is OUT of the stream (internal only)

These never appear in the narrative, even disguised as prose:

- **GM intentions and plans** — *"I'm setting up an ambush", "to raise the tension", "this will
  pay off later".* Show the world; do not announce your craft.
- **Hidden NPC internal states** — thoughts, secret motives, undisclosed plans, emotions no one in
  the scene can perceive. Show the *symptom* (a clenched jaw), never the *cause* (the fear behind
  it).
- **Off-screen / secret information** — events the present PCs did not witness, facts no one told
  them, contents of a sealed letter, what a faction is doing elsewhere.
- **Mechanics and meta** — DCs, thresholds, dice math, faction clocks, "session", file names,
  scripts. (Already covered by the Player Channel rule in `SOUL.md` — restated here for one place.)

> Rule of thumb: if a present PC could not produce the sentence by reporting their own senses, it
> does not belong in the stream. When in doubt, **externalize** (turn the hidden cause into a
> visible sign) or **stay vague** rather than narrate the secret.

## Filled Example

> *Context: the PCs enter a smith's workshop at dusk to ask after a missing courier. Internally,
> the GM knows the smith lied to the courier's pursuers that morning and is now afraid — but the
> PCs have no way to know any of this.*

```
The forge has gone cold; only embers remain, and the iron smell hangs heavy in the dark.
Hadrec the smith looks up from the bench, hammer still in hand. "We're closed," he says —
then his eyes flick to the road behind you before settling back on your faces.
A half-finished horseshoe lies abandoned on the anvil, the quench-bucket beside it untouched.

What do you do?
```

Note: the smith's fear is **shown** (the glance to the road, the abandoned work), never stated.
His morning lie and his fear stay internal. The PCs get a sensory scene and an opening.

## Counter-example — Telling → Showing

❌ **Telling** (intentions, hidden states, off-screen info leaked):

```
To build suspense, I'll have the smith act nervous because he's hiding that he betrayed the
courier this morning and is terrified the pursuers sent you. He decides to get rid of you quickly.
Roll Insight (DC 14) to notice he's lying.
```

✅ **Showing** (same beat, PC-perceivable only):

```
Hadrec sets the hammer down a little too fast. "We're closed," he says, already half-turning back
to the forge — though the forge is dark and there is nothing left to work. He doesn't ask what you
want.

What do you do?
```

The rewrite drops the GM's intention ("to build suspense"), the hidden state ("terrified",
"decides"), the off-screen fact ("betrayed the courier this morning"), and the mechanic ("Roll
Insight DC 14") — keeping only what the PCs in the room actually see and hear.
